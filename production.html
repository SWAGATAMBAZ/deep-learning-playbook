⚠️ The #1 production bug: train/serve skew

Features computed one way in a training notebook (SQL over a full month) and another way online (a streaming approximation). Use a feature store or shared feature code, and log the features you actually served so you can train on them.

Making deep models fast and small

Quantisation

FP32 → INT8 (or 4-bit for LLMs). About 4× smaller and 2–4× faster with a small accuracy loss. Post-training, or quantisation-aware training.

Knowledge distillation

Train a small "student" to match a big "teacher's" soft probabilities. DistilBERT keeps ~97% of BERT's quality and is ~60% faster.

Pruning

Remove low-magnitude weights or whole heads/channels. Structured pruning gives real speed-ups.

Compilation & runtimes

ONNX Runtime, TensorRT, torch.compile; dynamic batching on the server (Triton, TorchServe).

Caching

Precompute embeddings (items, customers); cache frequent results; KV cache for LLMs.

Monitoring drift

🧒 In plain English

The world changes after you deploy: a new UPI feature, a festival sale, fraudsters changing tactics. Data drift: the inputs look different. Concept drift: the same inputs now mean something different (the relation to the target changed). Labels often arrive weeks later, so you watch the inputs and the score distribution as early warnings.

Population Stability Index (PSI)
Shift today's distribution of a feature (e.g. transaction amount) away from the training baseline. Rule of thumb: PSI < 0.1 is stable, 0.1–0.25 needs watching, > 0.25 means act.
📐 PSI
PSI = Σbins (ai − ei) · ln(ai / ei)

e = expected (training) share per bin, a = actual (production) share. Also monitor the prediction distribution, missing rates, latency, and the business KPI. Once labels arrive, track the real metric over time.

Calibration: are your probabilities honest?

A model with great AUC can still output 0.9 for things that are fraud only 40% of the time. This matters whenever probabilities feed decisions: expected loss = PD × LGD × EAD, bid prices, thresholds. Deep nets are often over-confident. Fix with temperature scaling (one parameter fitted on validation data), Platt scaling or isotonic regression. Check with reliability diagrams and the Brier score. Resampling or class weights also distort probabilities, so re-calibrate afterwards.

Fairness & regulation (lending, insurance, hiring)

  • Don't use protected attributes, and watch proxies (pincode can proxy religion or caste; first name can proxy gender).
  • Measure: approval-rate ratio across groups (disparate impact), equal opportunity (TPR parity), and calibration within groups.
  • Document: model cards, data lineage, validation reports for model-risk management (SR 11-7-style governance in banks; RBI and other regulators expect explainability for credit decisions).
  • Keep a human in the loop for adverse decisions, and provide reason codes.

Interview Q&A

Your fraud model's precision dropped 30% over two months. How do you investigate?

Check the pipeline first (feature nulls, schema changes, upstream outages, train/serve skew). Then look at data drift per feature (PSI) and score-distribution shift. Slice performance by segment, channel and merchant to localise the drop. Check for concept drift (a new fraud pattern) through analyst feedback. Fix: retrain on recent data, add features for the new pattern, adjust the threshold, and set up automated drift alerts and a retraining cadence.

How do you serve a BERT classifier under 20 ms on CPU?

Distil to a smaller model (MiniLM/DistilBERT), shorten max_length, quantise to INT8, export to ONNX Runtime, use dynamic batching, cache frequent inputs. Benchmark p95, not the mean.

Why aren't attention weights a reliable explanation?

Attention shows where information was mixed in one layer, not what caused the output. Different attention patterns can give the same prediction, and value vectors and later layers transform things further. Use gradient-based attributions (Integrated Gradients) or SHAP for faithful explanations.

How do you A/B test a new model safely?

Shadow mode first (score but don't act) to compare distributions and latency. Then a small randomised traffic split with a pre-registered primary metric, guardrails (false declines, latency, complaints), power analysis for sample size, and a ramp-up plan with rollback.
