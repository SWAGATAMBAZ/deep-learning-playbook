Time-series forecasting with deep learning

Forecasting ATM cash, product demand across 50,000 SKUs, call-centre load or a customer's next-month spend. Deep learning shines when you have many related series and rich covariates, and you train one global model across all of them.

🎯 Interview point

In the M5 forecasting competition (Walmart sales), gradient-boosted trees on lag and calendar features dominated the leaderboard. Say this to show judgement: start with seasonal-naive and LightGBM baselines; reach for DL when you have scale, covariates or need distributions.

Framing: turn a series into supervised windows

🧒 In plain English

Slide a window along the history: "given the last 28 days (the lookback), predict the next 7 (the horizon)". Each slide is one training example. Add features you know in advance (day of week, holidays, planned promos) and static ones (store size, region).

  • Recursive: predict one step, feed it back, repeat. Errors compound.
  • Direct / multi-output: predict all H steps at once. This is standard for DL models.
  • Scale per series (divide by the series mean, or use log1p) so one global model can learn across big and small stores.

Models worth knowing

RNN

LSTM / GRU seq2seq

Encoder reads the history, decoder emits the horizon. A solid baseline that is sensitive to scaling.

CNN

TCN

Causal dilated 1D convolutions give an exponentially growing receptive field. Parallel and fast.

Amazon

DeepAR

A global autoregressive RNN that outputs a distribution (e.g. negative binomial for counts), not a point. Good for intermittent retail demand.

MLP

N-BEATS / N-HiTS

Stacks of MLP blocks that each forecast and subtract their fit from the input (backcast). Simple, fast and strong. N-BEATS can decompose into trend and seasonality.

Google

Temporal Fusion Transformer

Variable-selection networks + LSTM + attention + quantile outputs. Handles static, known-future and observed covariates, with interpretable feature weights. Popular in industry.

Transformer

PatchTST

Chops a series into patches (like ViT) and models each channel independently. Strong on long horizons.

Foundation

Chronos / TimesFM / Moirai

Pre-trained on huge collections of series and forecast zero-shot. A great quick baseline; fine-tune if needed.

Validation: never shuffle time

Walk-forward (expanding window) validation
Each fold trains on the past and tests on the next block, exactly like production. Switch to "random K-fold" to see the leakage: the model trains on the future.
⚠️ Leakage traps

Rolling features computed with a centred window. Scaling with statistics from the full series. Using a covariate you won't know at prediction time (actual weather, actual promo uptake). Target-encoding with future data. Add a gap between train and test if your features need lag time to arrive.

Metrics

MetricNotes
MAE / RMSEScale-dependent. RMSE punishes big misses.
MAPEExplodes when actuals are near 0 and penalises over-forecasting more. Avoid for intermittent demand.
WAPE = Σ|e| / Σ|y|The retail favourite: robust and business-readable.
MASEError relative to a seasonal-naive forecast. Below 1 means you beat naive.
Pinball / quantile lossFor P10/P50/P90 forecasts, e.g. safety stock at P90.
📐 Quantile (pinball) loss
Lq(y, ŷ) = max( q(y − ŷ), (q − 1)(y − ŷ) )

At q = 0.9, under-forecasting costs 9× more than over-forecasting, so the model learns to predict high: the 90th percentile. That's exactly what you want for "how much cash to load into this ATM".

Interview Q&A

Why can't you use regular K-fold CV for time series?

It trains on future data to predict the past, which leaks information (trends, regime changes) and gives optimistic scores. Use walk-forward / expanding or sliding-window splits that respect time order, with a gap if needed.

Local vs global models?

Local means one model per series (ARIMA per SKU): no sharing, bad for short or new series. Global means one model across all series. It learns shared patterns, handles cold-start series via static features and scales to millions of series. DL forecasters and LightGBM are typically global.

How would you forecast demand for a brand-new product?

There's no history, so use a global model with static and content features (category, price, brand, image/text embeddings), plus analogous products' launch curves. Start with category-level priors and update quickly as sales arrive.

What is TFT and why is it popular?

The Temporal Fusion Transformer combines gated variable-selection networks (which features matter), LSTM local processing, multi-head attention for long-range patterns, and quantile outputs. It natively handles static, known-future and past-observed covariates, and it's interpretable through variable weights and attention.

Why might MAPE be a bad choice?

It's undefined or explosive for zero or near-zero actuals and asymmetric (over-forecasts are penalised more than under-forecasts). Prefer WAPE, MASE or a cost-based metric tied to stockouts vs holding cost.
