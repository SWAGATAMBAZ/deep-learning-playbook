Anomaly detection & graph neural networks

Fraud is rare, labels arrive late, and fraudsters work in networks. Two deep learning tools appear again and again in fintech: autoencoders to flag "this doesn't look normal", and GNNs to catch "this account is connected to bad actors".

Autoencoders: learn "normal", flag the rest

🧒 In plain English

Train a network to squeeze a transaction into a tiny summary (the bottleneck) and rebuild it. Train only on normal transactions. It becomes very good at rebuilding normal ones and bad at rebuilding anything it has never seen. So a high reconstruction error = suspicious. You need no fraud labels at all.

📐 Structure
x → encoder → z (small) → decoder → x̂    score = ‖x − x̂‖²

If the bottleneck were as wide as the input, the network could just copy the input. The narrow z forces it to learn the main structure of normal data. It's like a non-linear PCA.

Variational autoencoder (VAE), briefly

A VAE's encoder outputs a distribution (mean μ and variance σ²) instead of a point. You sample z using the reparameterisation trick z = μ + σ·ε so gradients can flow through the sampling. The loss is reconstruction + a KL penalty that keeps the latent space close to N(0, I). That smooth latent space lets you generate new samples (synthetic data) and gives a probabilistic anomaly score. See Generative AI.

🎯 Interview point

Always benchmark against Isolation Forest and simple rules. Autoencoders win on high-dimensional or sequential inputs. With labels available (even a few), a supervised model on those labels plus the AE score as a feature usually beats either alone.

Graph neural networks: "you are who you're connected to"

🧒 In plain English

A mule account may look perfectly normal on its own. But it shares a device with three flagged accounts and receives money from a known scam wallet. A GNN lets each node gather messages from its neighbours, then neighbours of neighbours, so a node's representation absorbs its neighbourhood's risk. Fraud rings light up as connected clusters.

📐 Message passing (one layer)
hv(k+1) = σ( W · AGG({ hu(k) : u ∈ N(v) }) + B · hv(k) )

Each layer = one hop. Two or three layers is typical. Too many and all nodes start to look the same (over-smoothing).

Where they're used in fintech

Fraud rings

Accounts linked by device, IP, phone, address. Node classification on the account graph.

AML / mule detection

Money-flow graphs: fan-in/fan-out, rapid layering cycles. Edge and subgraph patterns.

Credit risk

Supply-chain or guarantor networks for SME lending, where defaults propagate.

Recommendations

User–item bipartite graphs (PinSage, LightGCN).

⚠️ Practical traps

Temporal leakage: build the graph only from edges that existed at prediction time. Label leakage through neighbours whose fraud labels were confirmed later. Latency: precompute embeddings in batch and update incrementally, or use GraphSAGE for new nodes. Many teams simply feed graph features (degree, PageRank, embeddings) into a GBDT.

Interview Q&A

How do you detect fraud with no labels?

Unsupervised anomaly detection: Isolation Forest, autoencoder reconstruction error, density or distance methods. Then use analyst feedback to build labels over time and move to semi-supervised or supervised learning. Include rules for known patterns. Evaluate with precision on top-K alerts against investigator capacity.

Why might an autoencoder fail to detect anomalies?

If it's too powerful (wide bottleneck, overfit), it reconstructs anomalies well too. If the training "normal" data is contaminated with fraud, it learns fraud as normal. Fixes: a narrower bottleneck, regularisation (denoising or sparse AE), cleaner training data, and scoring per-feature errors.

What is over-smoothing in GNNs?

With many message-passing layers, each node aggregates from nearly the whole graph, so embeddings converge and become indistinguishable. Use 2–3 layers, residual or jumping-knowledge connections, or attention.

Transductive vs inductive GNNs?

Transductive (vanilla GCN) learns on a fixed graph and can't directly embed unseen nodes. Inductive (GraphSAGE, GAT) learns an aggregation function that can embed new nodes from their features and neighbours. That's essential for fraud, where new accounts appear constantly.
