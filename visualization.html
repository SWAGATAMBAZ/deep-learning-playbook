Gradients and activations: look inside

🎯 Healthy signs to look for

Activation std roughly constant across layers (not shrinking to 0 or exploding). Dead-ReLU fraction not climbing towards 100%. Gradient norms similar in magnitude across layers. Update-to-weight ratio (lr·‖grad‖ / ‖w‖) around 1e-3: much smaller means the LR is too low, much larger means it's too high.

Interview Q&A

What do you log during training, at minimum?

Train and val loss plus the main metric per epoch, learning rate, global gradient norm, and periodically weight and gradient histograms. Also a sample grid of inputs after augmentation, and the confusion matrix / PR curve on validation. Log hyperparameters and the git commit so runs are comparable.

Why prefer the PR curve over ROC for fraud?

With 0.1–1% positives, the false-positive rate stays tiny even when false positives outnumber true frauds, so ROC looks great. Precision directly shows what fraction of alerts are real, which is what analysts and customers feel. Always compare AP with the prevalence baseline.

How do you check whether a CNN uses the right evidence?

Grad-CAM or occlusion maps on a sample of correct and incorrect predictions, sliced by class. If heatmaps sit on backgrounds, watermarks or text overlays, you have shortcut learning. Fix the data (crop, randomise backgrounds, add counter-examples) and re-check.

How do you know your model's probabilities are trustworthy?

Reliability diagram (predicted probability bins vs observed frequency), Brier score and expected calibration error on held-out data. If the curve sits below the diagonal (over-confident), apply temperature scaling or isotonic calibration on a validation set.

t-SNE showed perfectly separated clusters. Does that mean the classifier is great?

Not necessarily. t-SNE exaggerates cluster separation and its distances between clusters aren't meaningful; results depend on perplexity and seed. Use it for qualitative inspection only, and judge performance with held-out metrics.
