Recurrent neural networks (RNN)

An RNN reads a sequence one step at a time and carries a hidden state, a running summary of everything it has seen so far. It's the foundation for LSTM and GRU, and the place where the most famous deep learning problem shows up: vanishing gradients.

Why sequences need memory

🧒 In plain English

Think of reading a bank statement line by line. To judge whether today's ₹80,000 transfer is odd, you need to remember what came before: small UPI payments, salary on the 1st, nothing overseas. An MLP sees one fixed-size input and has no memory. An RNN keeps a notebook (the hidden state) and updates it after reading each line. It uses the same update rule at every step, so it works for sequences of any length.

Problem with a plain MLPHow an RNN fixes it
Needs a fixed input sizeProcesses one step at a time, so any length works
Learns "position 3 = amount" separately for every positionWeight sharing across time: one set of weights for all steps
No notion of orderThe hidden state is built in order, so order matters

The RNN cell

📐 The recurrence (memorise this)
ht = tanh( Wxh xt + Whh ht−1 + b )     ŷt = Why ht + by
  • xt: input at step t (size I), e.g. [amount, MCC embedding, hour].
  • ht−1: previous hidden state (size H), starting from h0 = 0.
  • Wxh (H×I) reads the new input. Whh (H×H) decides how the old memory is carried forward. Both are shared over all time steps.
  • tanh squashes to (−1, 1) so the state doesn't blow up when it's fed back in.
🧒 "Unrolling"

Draw the loop out over time and you get a very deep feed-forward network with one layer per time step, where every layer shares the same weights. A 100-step sequence means a 100-layer-deep computation. That's why RNNs inherit all the problems of very deep networks.

Step-through simulator

A one-neuron RNN reading a sequence
ht = tanh(wh·ht−1 + wx·xt + b). Choose an input pattern and watch how long the memory of the early spike survives. A small wh forgets fast; wh above 1 saturates and gets stuck.

Training: backpropagation through time (BPTT)

🧒 In plain English

Unroll the network over the sequence, compute the loss (at the end for many-to-one, or summed over every step for many-to-many), then run ordinary backprop backwards through all the time steps. Because the weights are shared, the gradient for Whh is the sum of its gradients from every step.

📐 Why long sequences are hard
∂ht/∂hk = ∏j=k+1..t diag(tanh′(aj)) · Whh

The gradient linking step k to step t is a product of (t − k) Jacobians. If the largest singular value of Whh times tanh′ is below 1, it shrinks exponentially and vanishes. Above 1, it explodes. Since tanh′ ≤ 1, vanishing is the usual case.

Truncated BPTT: backpropagate only through the last k steps (e.g. 50–200) of a long stream to save memory, while still carrying the hidden state forward. The model then can't learn dependencies longer than k through gradients.

Vanishing and exploding gradients

How much does step 1 still influence step T?
Gradient factor flowing back k steps (log scale). RNN: (w·tanh′)k. LSTM: cell path ≈ fk (forget gate). GRU: direct path ≈ zk (update gate keeping the old state). Gates the model can learn to keep near 1 are what fix the problem.
ProblemSymptomFixes
ExplodingLoss jumps to NaN or spikes; huge gradient normsGradient clipping by norm (0.5–5; 1.0 typical), lower LR, orthogonal init of Whh
VanishingThe model ignores anything more than ~10–20 steps back; early layers or steps get almost no gradientGated cells (LSTM/GRU), skip/residual connections over time, attention, identity/orthogonal init (IRNN), shorter effective sequences (aggregate events), or switch to TCN/transformers

PyTorch API and tensor shapes (a frequent source of bugs)

TensorShape (batch_first=True)Meaning

h_0 (optional)(num_layers × D, B, hidden_size)Initial state; zeros if omitted. D = 2 if bidirectional
output(B, T, D × hidden_size)Top layer's hidden state at every step. Use for many-to-many.

⚠️ The classic padding bug

With padded sequences, output[:, -1] is the state after reading padding for every shorter sequence. Use pack_padded_sequence and take h_n[-1], or gather the output at index length − 1 for each sequence.

Variants and extensions

Stacked (deep) RNN

Layer 2 reads layer 1's outputs. 2–3 layers usually help; more rarely does. Add dropout between layers (the dropout argument).

Bidirectional

One RNN reads forwards and another backwards; the outputs are concatenated (size 2H). Great when the whole sequence is known (NER, classifying a finished session). Not for real-time scoring or forecasting, because it would peek at the future.

Encoder–decoder (seq2seq)

An encoder compresses the input; a decoder generates the output step by step from the encoder's final state. Teacher forcing feeds the true previous token during training; beam search keeps the top-k partial outputs at inference.

Attention on top of an RNN

Instead of squeezing everything into hT, the decoder (or a classifier) takes a learned weighted average of all hidden states. This fixed the bottleneck and led directly to transformers.

Stateful RNN

Carry hT from one batch into the next for very long streams (sensor data, a customer's lifetime of events). Call h = h.detach() between batches, otherwise backprop runs through the whole history.

Gated RNNs

Add learnable gates that decide what to keep and what to overwrite: LSTM (1997) and GRU (2014). In practice you almost always use one of these instead of a vanilla RNN.

Preparing sequence data (where most real problems are)

  • Define the sequence: per customer, the last N events before the prediction time. Never include events after the prediction timestamp; this is the most common leakage in sequence models.
  • Choose N (lookback) from the business rhythm (e.g. 30 days or 50 transactions) and validate a few options.
  • Per-step features: numeric (log amount, standardised), categorical → embeddings (MCC, merchant, channel), and time-delta since the previous event (RNNs see order, not elapsed time).
  • Padding and masking: pad with a reserved index 0, pack sequences, and mask the loss on padded steps for many-to-many tasks.
  • Bucketing: batch sequences of similar length together to cut wasted padding compute.
  • Splits: split by time (and by customer if you need to generalise to new customers).

Interview Q&A

What is the hidden state in an RNN?

A fixed-size vector that summarises the sequence seen so far. At each step it's recomputed from the new input and the previous state with the same weights: ht = tanh(Wxhxt + Whhht−1 + b). It's the network's working memory.

Why do RNNs share weights across time steps?

So the same pattern is recognised wherever it occurs in the sequence, the parameter count doesn't grow with length, and the model can handle sequences longer than any seen in training. It's the time-axis version of a CNN sharing filters across space.

Explain BPTT and truncated BPTT.

BPTT unrolls the RNN over the sequence and applies ordinary backprop. The shared-weight gradients are summed over steps. Truncated BPTT only backpropagates through the last k steps while still carrying the state forward. It saves memory and time, but the model can't learn dependencies longer than k through gradients.

Why do vanilla RNNs suffer from vanishing gradients?

The gradient from step t back to step k is a product of t − k Jacobians Whhᵀ·diag(tanh′). With tanh′ ≤ 1 and typical weights, its norm shrinks exponentially, so early steps get almost no learning signal and long-range dependencies aren't learned. If the norm exceeds 1, gradients explode instead.

How do you handle exploding vs vanishing gradients?

Exploding: gradient clipping (by norm), a lower LR, orthogonal initialisation. Vanishing: change the architecture with gated cells (LSTM/GRU), attention, residual connections, or TCNs/transformers. Clipping does nothing for vanishing gradients.

output vs h_n in PyTorch?

output is the top layer's hidden state at every time step, shape (B, T, D·H) with batch_first. h_n is the last-step state of every layer and direction, shape (L·D, B, H), and it stays layer-first even with batch_first. Use output for tagging and h_n[-1] (with packing) for sequence classification.

When is a bidirectional RNN inappropriate?

Whenever predictions must be made before the future is known: real-time fraud scoring, next-event prediction, forecasting, streaming speech. The backward pass would leak future information.

What's teacher forcing and what problem does it create?

During seq2seq training the decoder is fed the ground-truth previous token rather than its own prediction. Training is faster and more stable, but at inference the model sees its own mistakes, which it never practised on (exposure bias). Mitigations: scheduled sampling, or beam search at inference.

RNN vs 1D-CNN/TCN vs transformer for sequences?

RNNs process steps sequentially and have O(1) streaming updates, which is good for real-time and small data, but they train slowly and struggle with long range. TCNs are parallel with a fixed receptive field. Transformers are parallel, connect any two positions directly and scale with data and pretraining, but cost O(n²) in sequence length.
