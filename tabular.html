Deep learning on tabular data

Most data at banks and fintechs lives in tables: customers, transactions, loans. The honest truth interviewers want to hear is that gradient-boosted trees are still the default, and deep learning earns its place only in specific situations.

Why trees usually beat neural nets on tables

🧒 In plain English

Tabular features are a mixed bag: age, income, merchant category, "days since last login". Each column means something different, and the rules are often sharp ("if utilisation > 90% then risk jumps"). Trees are built to make exactly those sharp, axis-aligned cuts. Neural networks prefer smooth functions and treat every input the same way, so they need more data and tuning to match trees.

  • Trees are robust to uninformative features and to feature scale. MLPs get distracted by noise columns.
  • Target functions on tables are often irregular or step-like, and NNs are biased toward smooth solutions.
  • XGBoost, LightGBM and CatBoost handle missing values and categoricals natively and train in minutes on a CPU.

Entity embeddings: the key DL trick for tables

🧒 In plain English

One-hot encoding 50,000 merchant IDs gives 50,000 sparse columns that know nothing about each other. An embedding gives each merchant a small learned vector, say 16 numbers, so merchants that behave alike (two grocery chains) end up close together. The model learns this map while it trains, and you can reuse the vectors in other models, including XGBoost.

📐 What it really is

An embedding layer is a lookup table, a matrix E of shape (n_categories × d). E[merchant_id] is mathematically identical to one-hot × E, just much faster. A common rule of thumb for d is fastai's round(1.6 · n0.56), capped at 600 in fastai. Many teams cap it lower (~50) for tabular models.

🎯 Interview point

"How do you handle a categorical with 1M levels?" Use an embedding, or hash it into buckets and then embed, or use target encoding with out-of-fold means for GBDTs. Mention an "unknown" index for unseen categories at inference time (the cold start problem).

✅ What actually works in industry

A hybrid: train a sequence model or autoencoder on raw transactions, then feed its embeddings as features into a GBDT. Or ensemble a GBDT with an MLP/FT-Transformer. Their errors are fairly uncorrelated, so blending them usually adds a few points of AUC.

Preprocessing checklist for tabular NNs

  • Scale numericals: standardise, or use QuantileTransformer/log for skewed amounts. Trees don't care about scale; NNs care a lot.
  • Missing values: impute and add an "is_missing" flag column.
  • Categoricals: integer-encode, then embed. Reserve index 0 for unknown/rare values.
  • Split by time for anything behavioural, to avoid leakage.
  • Fit scalers on train only.

Interview Q&A

Your manager wants a neural net for credit default. You have 200k rows and 60 features. What do you say?

Start with a tuned LightGBM/XGBoost baseline. It will probably be as good or better, it's faster, and it's easier to explain to model-risk and regulators (SHAP, monotonic constraints, adverse-action reasons). Try an MLP/FT-Transformer only if there's a signal trees can't use (raw transaction sequences, text, very high-cardinality IDs), and keep it only if it beats the baseline on out-of-time validation by enough to justify the extra complexity.

What are embeddings and why are they better than one-hot?

They're dense, low-dimensional learned vectors per category. Compared with one-hot: far fewer parameters, they capture similarity between categories, they generalise to rare categories by sharing structure, and they're reusable as features elsewhere.

How does TabNet provide interpretability?

At each decision step, an attentive transformer produces a sparse mask (via sparsemax) over the input features. Aggregating the masks gives per-row (local) and global feature importances, so you can see which features the model attended to for a specific prediction.

Why must you scale features for neural networks but not for trees?

Trees split on thresholds, so monotonic transforms don't change them. NNs compute weighted sums and gradients. Unscaled features (amount in lakhs next to a 0/1 flag) create badly conditioned loss surfaces, which means slow, unstable training and saturated activations.

How do you combine a GBDT and a neural net?

(1) Blend: average the calibrated probabilities, or stack with a logistic-regression meta-model. (2) Feature hand-off: feed NN embeddings (merchant vectors, sequence encodings) into the GBDT. (3) Use GBDT leaf indices as categorical inputs to the NN (the Facebook GBDT+LR idea).
