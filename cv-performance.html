The vision performance playbook

"Your image model is at 82%. Get it to 90%." This is the most common practical CV interview question. Here's the full toolbox: diagnose first, then pull data, augmentation, training-recipe, architecture and inference levers, roughly in order of payoff.

Step 2: data levers (usually the biggest win)

labels

Fix label noise

Review the highest-loss training images; they're often mislabelled. Tools like cleanlab rank suspicious labels. A few percent of noisy labels can cost several points of accuracy.

coverage

Cover the long tail

Slice errors by device, lighting, region and category. Collect data for the worst slices, not more of what you already have.

active learning

Label smartly

Label the images the model is least sure about (lowest max-probability, highest entropy, ensemble disagreement). You get more accuracy per labelled image than with random sampling.

self-supervised

Use unlabelled images

Start from DINOv3 or CLIP features, or pretrain with MAE / SimCLR on your own unlabelled domain images, then fine-tune.

pseudo-labels

Semi-supervised

Train, predict on unlabelled data, keep high-confidence predictions as labels, retrain (Noisy Student, FixMatch).

synthetic

Generate data

Copy-paste objects onto backgrounds, render 3D, or use diffusion. Use it for rare cases, and always validate on real data.

⚠️ Augmentation rules

Augment training only; validation and test get deterministic resize + normalise. For detection and segmentation, transform boxes and masks with the image. Stronger augmentation needs longer training. Scale augmentation strength with model size: big models benefit from heavy augmentation, tiny models can be hurt by it.

Step 4: the modern fine-tuning recipe

  1. Start from the best pretrained weights you can use (licence!). Strong self-supervised or large-data pretraining beats a bigger architecture trained on less.
  2. Two-phase fine-tuning: train the new head with the backbone frozen (1–5 epochs, LR ~1e-3), then unfreeze and train everything with a low LR (1e-5 to 1e-4).
  3. Discriminative LRs / layer-wise LR decay: earlier layers get smaller LRs (e.g. ×0.65–0.75 per block for ViTs) because they hold general features.
  4. AdamW + warm-up + cosine decay; weight decay 0.05 for ViTs, ~1e-4 to 0.05 for CNNs.
  5. Regularise: label smoothing 0.1, MixUp/CutMix, drop-path (stochastic depth) 0.1–0.2, RandAugment.
  6. EMA of weights: keep a moving average of the weights (decay ~0.999–0.9999) and evaluate with it. A free +0.2–1%.
  7. Resolution: train at a lower resolution and fine-tune the last epochs at a higher one (progressive resizing / FixRes). Higher test resolution often helps.
  8. Mixed precision (bf16/fp16), channels_last, torch.compile: 1.5–3× faster training means more experiments.
  9. Batch norm with tiny batches: freeze BN statistics, or use GroupNorm/LayerNorm, or accumulate gradients.

Step 5: inference-time gains

  • Test-time augmentation (TTA): average predictions over flips or multi-scale crops. +0.5–2%, costs N× compute.
  • Ensembles: different seeds, architectures (CNN + ViT) or resolutions. Reliable gains; distil into one model for production.
  • Model soups: average the weights of several fine-tuned runs from the same init. Ensemble-like gains at single-model cost.
  • Per-class thresholds and calibration: tune on validation data for your business cost.
  • Reject option: route low-confidence images to humans (common in KYC and insurance).

Interview Q&A

Your image classifier is at 82% and needs 90%. Walk me through it.

First, error analysis: confusion matrix, worst classes, view the misclassified images. Check for label noise and train/val leakage (near-duplicate images). Check bias vs variance. If variance: stronger augmentation (RandAugment, MixUp/CutMix), better pretrained backbone, regularisation, more or targeted data via active learning. If bias: bigger or better backbone, higher resolution, longer training, tuned LR. Then recipe tricks (EMA, label smoothing, layer-wise LR decay), and finally TTA and ensembling or distillation. Measure each change in isolation.

MixUp vs CutMix?

MixUp blends whole images pixel-wise, λ·x₁ + (1−λ)·x₂, with labels mixed by λ. It smooths decision boundaries and improves calibration, but images look unnatural. CutMix pastes a rectangular patch from one image into another, with labels mixed by area. It keeps local realism and forces the model to use multiple regions. Both regularise; they're often alternated randomly.

Why would horizontal flip hurt a model?

When orientation carries meaning: text and digits (OCR, cheques), left/right organs, traffic signs with arrows, chirality in chemistry. The augmentation must preserve the label.

Why freeze the backbone first, then unfreeze?

The new head starts random, so its large early gradients would flow back and damage pretrained features (catastrophic forgetting). Training the head first gives it sensible weights. Then unfreezing with a low LR, smaller still for early layers, adapts the features gently.

What is EMA of weights and why does it help?

Keep θ_ema ← d·θ_ema + (1−d)·θ after each step. It averages the weights over the end of training, landing in a flatter, more central region of the loss surface. That gives better generalisation and a more stable checkpoint at almost no cost.

How do you detect shortcut learning?

Grad-CAM or occlusion maps on correct predictions (is it looking at the object or the watermark?), test on slices where the shortcut is absent, and swap backgrounds. Classic example: a pneumonia model learned the hospital's scanner tokens.

Model works on validation but fails on customers' phone photos. Why and fix?

Domain shift: different cameras, blur, compression, lighting, framing. Collect a labelled sample of real production images for validation, augment to match (JPEG compression, blur, perspective, colour), fine-tune on in-domain data, add a quality gate (reject blurry or dark images with guidance to retake), and monitor input statistics.
